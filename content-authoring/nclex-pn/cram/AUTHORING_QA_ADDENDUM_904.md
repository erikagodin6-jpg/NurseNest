## 88 — pediatric emergency/toxicology safety
Ten lessons cover button-battery ingestion, hydrocarbon ingestion, iron overdose, acetaminophen overdose, salicylate toxicity, caustic ingestion, opioid exposure, cannabis-edible exposure, pediatric lead exposure/testing, and pediatric carbon-monoxide poisoning.

Safety protections:
- button-battery ingestion is time-critical even when initially asymptomatic;
- vomiting is not induced for button batteries, hydrocarbons, or caustics;
- formal poison-center guidance governs any battery mitigation and never delays emergency removal/evaluation;
- early wellness does not exclude acetaminophen toxicity;
- tachypnea can be compensatory in salicylate toxicity;
- naloxone response does not remove the need for recurrence monitoring;
- routine pulse oximetry can appear normal in carbon-monoxide poisoning;
- lead exposure is often asymptomatic and is identified through exposure history and blood testing.

## 89 — geriatric acute-change/polypharmacy safety
Ten lessons cover actual-use medication reconciliation, acute confusion after medication change, medication-associated falls, anticholinergic burden, renal decline/drug accumulation, deprescribing awareness, anticoagulant bleeding risk, hypoglycemia presenting as confusion/falls, dehydration-plus-medication risk, and high-risk OTC use.

Safety protections:
- OTC products and supplements are included in reconciliation;
- abrupt confusion is treated as an acute change rather than normal aging;
- a fall triggers medication and physiologic review, not only environmental correction;
- multiple anticholinergic medicines can create a combined toxicity syndrome;
- longstanding doses are reassessed when renal function changes;
- prescribing/deprescribing remains authorized prescriber work and some therapies require tapering;
- head trauma or subtle bleeding on anticoagulants is escalated;
- hypoglycemia may present as confusion, weakness, or falls;
- generic medication-hold rules are not invented for dehydration/acute illness;
- nonprescription status does not imply low medication risk.
