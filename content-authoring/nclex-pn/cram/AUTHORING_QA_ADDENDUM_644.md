## Nutrition / enteral / parenteral safety

Ten new lessons cover enteral tube placement verification, aspiration prevention, interrupted feeds, refeeding risk, parenteral-nutrition line safety, PN metabolic monitoring, tube-clog prevention, medication administration through feeding tubes, GI feeding tolerance, and home feeding-pump failure planning.

Safety rules preserved:
- uncertain tube position means stop and verify before use;
- respiratory deterioration overrides a reassuring isolated feeding metric;
- feeding interruptions trigger review of glucose-, fluid-, and medication-dependent therapies;
- severe malnutrition requires refeeding-risk recognition rather than rapid unmonitored calorie escalation;
- PN is treated as a high-risk vascular infusion with strict asepsis, line tracing, compatibility, glucose, electrolyte, and fluid monitoring;
- formulation rules remain in force for medication-through-tube administration;
- tube resistance is not a reason to apply force;
- home pump users require device-specific backup and escalation plans.

## Sensory / HEENT acute safety

Ten new lessons cover sudden vision loss, retinal-detachment cues, acute angle-closure glaucoma, chemical-eye exposure, orbital trauma/open-globe precautions, sudden hearing change, ototoxic exposure, ear-infection complications, post-cataract warning signs, and communication safety for hearing impairment.

Safety rules preserved:
- sudden vision loss is urgent even without pain;
- flashes/floaters with curtain-like field loss require urgent retinal evaluation;
- painful red eye with halos/nausea is treated as acute angle closure until evaluated;
- chemical eye exposure receives immediate irrigation without waiting for exact chemical identification;
- suspected open-globe injury is shielded without pressure and embedded objects are not removed;
- sudden unexplained hearing loss is not normalized as aging or wax;
- tinnitus and disequilibrium can be early ototoxicity cues;
- postoperative severe eye pain or vision loss is not routine recovery;
- communication access is treated as medication, consent, fall, and discharge safety.
