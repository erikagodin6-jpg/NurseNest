## New dialysis / renal-replacement safety batch

Ten lessons add hemodialysis access protection, intradialytic hypotension recognition, dialysis infection, fluid overload, hyperkalemia cues, peritoneal-dialysis peritonitis, poor PD outflow, post-dialysis bleeding, missed-dialysis deterioration, and dialysis-day medication timing.

High-risk rules preserved:
- AV fistula/graft access is protected from routine venipuncture/compression and absent flow is an urgent cue.
- Dialysis prescription, ultrafiltration, access cannulation, and catheter manipulation remain competency/policy dependent.
- Persistent bleeding is managed without sacrificing access patency.
- Cloudy PD effluent plus abdominal/systemic symptoms is escalated promptly.
- Chronic potassium binders are never represented as emergency stabilization for unstable hyperkalemia.
- Missed dialysis is triaged by patient stability and symptoms, not the calendar alone.
- Medication timing around dialysis is drug specific; no blanket hold rule is taught.

## New diabetes-device / glucose-monitoring safety batch

Ten lessons add CGM confirmation when symptoms and sensor values disagree, compression-low artifacts, sensor failure backup monitoring, pump interruption, infusion-site failure, sick-day ketone escalation, hypoglycemia alarm response, automated-insulin-delivery limitations, patient-dedicated device safety, and technology backup planning.

High-risk rules preserved:
- CGM readings do not override severe clinical symptoms when device accuracy is questionable.
- Backup glucose monitoring remains available for sensor failure or discrepancy.
- Basal insulin is not stopped merely because CGM or pump technology fails.
- Repeated corrections are not delivered through a suspected failed pump site.
- Severe hypoglycemia with unsafe swallow uses emergency rescue, not oral carbohydrate.
- Automated insulin delivery does not eliminate hypoglycemia, hyperglycemia, DKA, or device-failure risk.
- Insulin pens and patient-contact diabetes devices are not shared between patients.
- Backup insulin regimens must be prescribed; no conversion doses are invented during device failure.
