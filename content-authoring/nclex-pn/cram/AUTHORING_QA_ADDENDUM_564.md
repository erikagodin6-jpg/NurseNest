## Pediatric acute-care batch

Ten lessons add pediatric dehydration/oral rehydration, respiratory deterioration, febrile seizure safety, young-infant fever escalation, croup, airway foreign body, meningitis red flags, pediatric DKA recognition, pediatric sepsis recognition, and intussusception.

Safety rules preserved include: ORS for appropriate mild/moderate dehydration with escalation for shock/altered mental status/enteral failure; respiratory effort and mental status override false reassurance from one saturation value; nothing goes in the mouth during a seizure; young-infant fever is high risk; stridor at rest is an airway warning; no blind finger sweeps; do not wait for classic neck stiffness in a sick infant; DKA deep breathing is metabolic compensation; pediatric hypotension is a late shock sign; and bloody stool is not required before escalating suspected intussusception.

Current source refresh includes AAP 2025 oral-rehydration review, CDC pediatric respiratory emergency warning signs and febrile-seizure guidance, ADA Standards of Care in Diabetes—2026, and current U.S. pediatric emergency/sepsis principles.

## Gerontology / long-term-care batch

Ten lessons add delirium-vs-dementia, falls, dehydration, dysphagia/aspiration, pressure injury prevention, polypharmacy, atypical infection presentation, constipation/impaction, urinary retention, and acute functional decline.

Safety rules preserved include: acute confusion is not normalized as dementia; post-fall injury assessment precedes routine lifting; thirst is an unreliable dehydration screen in many older adults; aspiration can be silent; persistent bony-prominence erythema is not massaged; every new symptom warrants medication review; absence of fever does not exclude serious infection; overflow diarrhea can indicate impaction; small frequent voids can coexist with retention; and acute loss of ADL function is a clinical cue rather than 'normal aging.'

Current source refresh includes the CMS MDS 3.0 RAI Manual current through June 2026, AHRQ fall/pressure-injury prevention principles, CDC antimicrobial stewardship principles, and the 2026 NCLEX-PN blueprint.
