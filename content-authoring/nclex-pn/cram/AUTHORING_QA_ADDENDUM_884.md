## Vascular/peripheral-perfusion protections

The newest vascular batch covers acute limb ischemia, chronic limb-threatening ischemia, suspected DVT, pulmonary embolism, possible AAA rupture, post-revascularization deterioration, venous-ulcer compression safety, carotid-procedure deterioration, arterial-access hematoma, and acute change in chronic venous insufficiency.

Safety rules preserved:
- acute limb ischemia is escalated before pulse loss/paralysis;
- suspected DVT is not massaged;
- sudden dyspnea/pleuritic pain/syncope with VTE risk is treated as possible PE even with clear lung sounds;
- possible AAA rupture is treated as internal hemorrhage despite limited external blood loss;
- post-revascularization pain is paired with neurovascular reassessment rather than normalized;
- strong compression is not applied without an established arterial-safety plan;
- carotid-procedure care protects both neurologic status and airway;
- antithrombotic therapy is not independently stopped because bleeding is suspected;
- current source refresh includes the 2024 ACC/AHA lower-extremity PAD guideline and CDC VTE guidance.

## Gynecologic acute-care protections

The newest gynecologic batch covers ovarian torsion, acute abnormal uterine bleeding, PID, tubo-ovarian abscess, post-hysterectomy hemorrhage, vaginal cuff dehiscence, post-cervical-procedure bleeding/infection, sexual-assault care, Bartholin abscess, and heavy menstrual bleeding/bleeding-disorder recognition in adolescents.

Safety rules preserved:
- preserved Doppler flow does not automatically exclude torsion;
- acute uterine bleeding is prioritized by hemodynamic status;
- pregnancy/ectopic and surgical differentials remain relevant in acute pelvic pain;
- worsening PID despite treatment triggers abscess/sepsis evaluation;
- internal postoperative hemorrhage may exceed visible vaginal loss;
- exposed tissue after vaginal cuff dehiscence is protected, not pushed back;
- sexual-assault care remains trauma-informed and consent/jurisdiction dependent;
- heavy adolescent menses can indicate an inherited bleeding disorder rather than being dismissed as normal early cycles.
