## New source-family batches

### 80 — arterial-line/hemodynamic-monitoring safety
Ten lessons cover invasive-pressure accuracy, damped waveform/system troubleshooting, distal limb perfusion, disconnection/hemorrhage, arterial-vs-IV route protection, sampling/blood-loss safety, catheter infection, line tracing/misconnection prevention, cuff-versus-arterial discrepancy, and removal/post-removal bleeding.

Safety protections:
- assess the patient and waveform/system before treating an implausible monitor number;
- distal neurovascular compromise is escalated before pulse loss;
- arterial disconnection is treated as hemorrhage risk;
- arterial monitoring lines are never represented as routine medication routes;
- invasive-line calibration/zeroing, sampling, removal, repair and vasoactive titration remain competency/facility dependent;
- current CDC arterial-catheter/pressure-monitoring infection-control principles are preserved.

### 81 — noninvasive ventilation/high-flow safety
Ten lessons cover airway-protection suitability, mask leak/skin injury, vomiting/aspiration, NIV failure, COPD hypercapnic deterioration, HFNC failure monitoring, alarm response, claustrophobia without unsafe reflex sedation, humidification/comfort, and transport/escalation planning.

Safety protections:
- NIV is not allowed to delay definitive rescue in worsening respiratory failure;
- oxygen saturation alone is not used as proof of adequate ventilation;
- vomiting or declining airway protection changes NIV suitability immediately;
- sedation is not used simply to force mask tolerance;
- settings/escalation remain respiratory/medical competency dependent.

### 82 — renal/urologic procedure safety
Ten lessons cover kidney-biopsy bleeding and medication reconciliation, cystoscopy/ureteroscopy aftercare, ureteral-stent complications, lithotripsy obstruction/infection, TURP/CBI output logic, voiding failure after catheter removal, post-biopsy discharge, post-instrumentation infection, and hematuria/clot retention.

Safety protections:
- kidney-biopsy warning signs follow NIDDK bleeding/urinary red flags;
- anticoagulant/antiplatelet holds are not generalized;
- CBI output is interpreted after accounting for irrigant;
- fever plus obstructive urinary symptoms is escalated as high risk;
- manual irrigation/recatheterization/procedure-level interventions remain competency/order dependent.

### 83 — GI/endoscopy procedure safety
Ten lessons cover sedation monitoring/rescue, EGD perforation cues, colonoscopy-prep hydration/medication risk, post-polypectomy bleeding, ERCP pancreatitis, ERCP cholangitis/bleeding, paracentesis aftercare, PEG displacement/peritonitis, liver-biopsy bleeding, and procedure-specific discharge teaching.

Safety protections:
- severe progressive post-endoscopy pain is never normalized without complication assessment;
- post-ERCP fever, shock, bleeding, or severe pain requires urgent escalation;
- sedation roles remain credential/facility dependent and monitoring includes cardiopulmonary status and depth of sedation;
- medication hold/restart plans are individualized rather than templated.
