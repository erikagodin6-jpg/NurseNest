## Diagnostic / laboratory safety batch

Ten lessons add specimen identity/integrity, blood-culture quality, timed drug levels, point-of-care glucose verification, coagulation-specimen safety, urine-culture collection, stool-testing requirements, critical-result escalation, test-preparation verification, and hemolyzed-specimen recollection logic.

Required safety rules preserved:
- uncertain specimen identity fails closed to rejection/recollection;
- blood-culture contamination prevention cannot delay urgent sepsis treatment in unstable patients;
- timed drug levels require verified collection timing relative to dose;
- implausible point-of-care glucose results are interpreted with the patient and repeated/confirmed when appropriate;
- suspect coagulation specimens do not drive independent anticoagulant changes;
- urine cultures are not collected from drainage bags;
- stool testing follows test-specific specimen requirements;
- critical results require closed-loop communication and patient assessment;
- test preparation is procedure specific and does not justify blanket medication holds;
- hemolyzed specimens are not treated as unquestioned physiologic truth.

## Emergency / disaster / environmental safety batch

Ten lessons add heat stroke, hypothermia, carbon-monoxide poisoning, drowning, lightning injury, chemical exposure/decontamination, mass-casualty triage, generator safety, smoke inhalation, and disaster medication-continuity planning.

Required safety rules preserved:
- altered mental status with heat illness triggers emergency cooling/escalation;
- severe hypothermia is handled gently and rewarmed according to severity;
- normal pulse oximetry does not exclude carbon-monoxide poisoning;
- drowning resuscitation prioritizes oxygenation/ventilation rather than expelling water;
- lightning victims do not retain electrical charge and are safe to touch for rescue;
- chemical exposures require rescuer PPE and secondary-contamination control;
- disaster triage follows incident command and scarcity-aware prioritization;
- portable generators are never presented as safe in garages or other enclosed occupied spaces;
- enclosed-space smoke exposure can produce occult airway/CO injury despite small external burns;
- critical medication/device continuity is planned before supply-chain or power failure.
