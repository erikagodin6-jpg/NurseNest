## Postpartum safety batch

Ten lessons add postpartum hemorrhage, postpartum preeclampsia, endometritis, VTE, cesarean/perineal wound complications, urinary retention, lactational mastitis, postpartum depression, postpartum psychosis, and peripartum cardiomyopathy.

High-risk rules preserved:

- postpartum danger continues after discharge; delivery does not end preeclampsia, VTE, hemorrhage, infection, cardiomyopathy, or psychiatric risk;
- heavy bleeding plus instability outranks routine lochia teaching;
- severe headache, visual change, dyspnea, seizure, or severe hypertension requires urgent escalation;
- suspected DVT limbs are not massaged;
- postpartum psychosis is treated as an emergency with patient/infant safety protection;
- dyspnea, orthopnea, chest symptoms, or syncope are not dismissed as normal postpartum fatigue;
- uterotonic, magnesium, antihypertensive, anticoagulant, antibiotic, and psychiatric medication responsibilities remain state/facility specific.

Current source refresh includes ACOG postpartum hemorrhage and postpartum-care resources and CDC Hear Her urgent maternal warning signs current through 2026 review.

## Professional-practice and coordinated-care batch

Ten lessons add delegation vs assignment, chain of command, informed-consent boundaries, clinical documentation, incident reporting, HIPAA/privacy, qualified interpreter use, handoff communication, restraint safety, and discharge medication reconciliation.

High-risk rules preserved:

- delegation transfers a task, not nursing judgment; LPN/VN delegation authority varies by jurisdiction;
- unresolved safety concerns continue through the chain of command;
- witnessing a signature is not the same as obtaining procedure-specific informed consent;
- documentation is objective, attributable, timely, and never pre-charted;
- after an incident, assess/protect the patient before completing safety reporting;
- HIPAA does not prohibit necessary provider-to-provider treatment communication;
- qualified interpretation is used for high-stakes communication rather than relying on children or unqualified family members for convenience;
- handoff transfers changes, risks, pending results, and escalation triggers—not only tasks;
- restraints remain least-restrictive, time-limited, and continuously reassessed rather than routine fall prevention;
- discharge reconciliation explicitly resolves start/continue/change/stop medication instructions.

Current source refresh includes NCSBN delegation guidance, HHS HIPAA minimum-necessary/interpreter guidance, and Joint Commission current Right Patient, Right Care communication and safety principles.
