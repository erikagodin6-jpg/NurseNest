## New infection/isolation safety batch

Ten lessons add Standard, Contact, Droplet, and Airborne precautions; C. difficile control; invasive-device infection prevention; occupational blood exposure response; clean-versus-sterile technique; immunocompromised infection recognition; and multidrug-resistant-organism stewardship/isolation safety.

Required safety rules preserved:
- Standard Precautions apply to all patients and are exposure/task based.
- Transmission-Based Precautions add to Standard Precautions and remain pathogen/procedure specific.
- C. difficile teaching preserves enteric/contact and sporicidal environmental-control principles rather than relying on generic alcohol-hand-rub messaging alone.
- Airborne-risk care never substitutes a routine surgical mask for required respiratory protection.
- Invasive-device convenience is never an indication for continued device exposure.
- Needlestick/mucosal blood exposure is treated as time-sensitive occupational evaluation.
- Sterility uncertainty fails closed as contamination.
- Immunocompromised patients may have subtle infection cues; fever thresholds remain treatment-plan specific.
- Antibiotic stewardship does not delay urgent sepsis treatment and does not equate colonization with infection.

## New psychosocial-crisis safety batch

Ten lessons add immediate suicide safety, escalating violence/de-escalation, panic with medical-rule-out safeguards, trauma-informed care, intimate-partner violence, elder abuse, acute grief, delirium-related behavioral change, behavioral-restraint last-resort principles, and substance-intoxication/withdrawal deterioration.

Required safety rules preserved:
- Active suicide risk is never managed by a no-harm promise alone.
- Staff do not physically confront escalating violence without trained assistance and an exit/safety plan.
- First-time or atypical chest/respiratory symptoms are not dismissed as panic before medical danger is considered.
- Trauma-informed care preserves patient choice/privacy whenever clinically possible.
- Abuse screening is private and nonjudgmental; reporting duties remain jurisdiction dependent.
- Acute behavioral change in an older adult triggers medical/delirium evaluation before psychiatric attribution.
- Restraint/seclusion remains last-resort, policy-governed emergency safety care with monitoring—not punishment or convenience.
- Intoxication does not exclude hypoglycemia, head injury, hypoxia, overdose, or withdrawal.
