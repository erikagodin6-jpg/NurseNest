## Clinical standards

- Geriatric care is function-, frailty-, capacity-, life-expectancy- and goals-aware rather than age-based.
- Comprehensive assessment includes cognition, mood, mobility/falls, medications, continence, nutrition, sensory function, ADLs/IADLs, supports and caregiver burden.
- Frailty is reduced reserve, not chronological age; disability and dementia are distinct constructs.
- Falls are syndromic: orthostasis, medications, gait/strength, vision, syncope and environment are assessed before generic precautions.
- Polypharmacy review targets indication/benefit horizon, interactions, adverse effects and prescribing cascades; dependence/rebound-prone drugs are tapered safely.
- Dementia is chronic cognitive decline with functional impact and is not inevitable aging; delirium is acute/fluctuating brain failure and requires a cause search.
- Late-life depression/anxiety are treatable and not normalized as aging; chronic benzodiazepine/sedative burden is minimized.
- Incontinence/constipation/nutrition/sleep/pain lessons prioritize reversible contributors and function rather than adding medications reflexively.
- Osteoporosis uses Canadian fracture-risk assessment, falls and treatment sequencing; denosumab discontinuation requires a transition plan.
- Driving and elder-abuse decisions are function/capacity/safety/jurisdiction based; cognitive score or age alone is insufficient.
- Advance care planning is values/future decision-making, distinct from present consent; palliative care is need-based and can coexist with disease treatment.
- Hypertension Canada 2025 exceptions for frailty/falls/orthostasis are preserved; intensive BP control is not pursued when it causes standing harm.
- Diabetes Canada older-adult care uses function/frailty/dementia/life expectancy to individualize targets and strictly prevent hypoglycemia.
- Heart-failure therapy remains evidence-based but is titrated to congestion/perfusion, kidney function, orthostasis, frailty and goals.
- Cognitive/functional tools support decisions but do not themselves determine capacity.
- Transitions and LTC require medication reconciliation, named pending-result ownership, baseline function/cognition and goal-concordant transfer decisions.
