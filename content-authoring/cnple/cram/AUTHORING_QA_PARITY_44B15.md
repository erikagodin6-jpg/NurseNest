## Clinical audit

### GERD
PASS. Typical uncomplicated reflux can be treated empirically, but dysphagia, bleeding/anemia, weight loss or persistent vomiting trigger diagnostic evaluation. Extra-esophageal symptoms are not automatically labelled GERD.

### Peptic ulcer / H. pylori
PASS. Active-infection testing and post-treatment test-of-cure are explicit. Symptom improvement is not accepted as eradication proof. NSAID/antithrombotic risk and perforation/bleeding are addressed.

### Dyspepsia
PASS. Dyspepsia remains a symptom syndrome rather than a label of “gastritis.” Alarm features, H. pylori strategy and defined treatment-response checkpoints are explicit.

### IBD
PASS. Diagnosis requires objective inflammation/phenotype assessment. Infection is excluded when relevant. Repeated systemic corticosteroid bursts are not taught as maintenance therapy. Toxic megacolon, obstruction, abscess and severe colitis trigger escalation.

### IBS
PASS. IBS is taught as a positive diagnosis after a targeted safety screen, not as an endless exclusion diagnosis. Alarm features and celiac/IBD differentiation are preserved; indiscriminate colonoscopy and food-allergy panels are rejected.

### Hepatitis B/C
PASS. HBV is interpreted as a serology/DNA pattern. Anti-HCV does not equal current infection; HCV RNA confirms viremia. HBV reactivation risk before immunosuppression and HCV DAA interaction review are explicit.

### MASLD/MASH
PASS. The legacy Full slug is preserved only for identity; learner-facing terminology uses MASLD/MASH. Fibrosis risk—not ALT alone or the presence of steatosis—drives referral and prognosis. Normal ALT does not exclude advanced disease.

### Cirrhosis complications
PASS. Decompensation is complication-driven. Diagnostic paracentesis/SBP evaluation, variceal bleeding, HE, AKI and transplant/hepatology referral are explicit. Serum ammonia and routine INR correction traps are rejected.

### Pancreatitis
PASS. Pancreatitis is a clinical syndrome rather than a lipase number. Early CT and prophylactic antibiotics are not routine. Fluids are reassessed rather than delivered as a fixed universal volume, and biliary obstruction/cholangitis changes urgency.

### Gallbladder disease
PASS. Biliary colic, cholecystitis, choledocholithiasis and cholangitis are separated. Cholangitis is a source-control emergency; ERCP is not taught as a routine diagnostic test for simple biliary colic.

### Colorectal cancer screening
PASS. Screening, surveillance and diagnostic colonoscopy are separated. FIT is not used to rule out cancer in a symptomatic patient; provincial/territorial implementation variability is explicit.

### Chronic diarrhea
PASS. Inflammatory, watery and malabsorptive mechanisms are separated. Medication causes, celiac/IBD/microscopic colitis and alarm features are preserved. Antidiarrheals are not used reflexively in severe inflammatory disease.

### GI bleeding
PASS. Hemodynamics and perfusion come before localization and hemoglobin. A normal initial hemoglobin does not reassure an unstable patient. Variceal vs nonvariceal pathways and antithrombotic risk are distinguished.

### Abdominal pain
PASS. Disposition precedes diagnostic perfection. Peritonitis, shock, pregnancy emergencies, ischemia, obstruction/perforation and vascular catastrophes are explicit; analgesia is not withheld to “preserve the exam.”

### Liver-test interpretation
PASS. Injury, cholestasis and synthetic function are different axes. AST is not liver-specific, normal aminotransferases do not exclude advanced fibrosis, and falling enzymes do not prove recovery when INR/mental status worsens.
