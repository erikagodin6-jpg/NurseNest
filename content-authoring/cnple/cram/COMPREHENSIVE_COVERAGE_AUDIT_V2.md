# CNPLE Cram Library — Comprehensive Coverage and Standards Audit V2

Audit date: 2026-08-07
Branch: `content/cnple-cram-authoring-20260807`
Clinical scope: Canadian Nurse Practitioner entry-to-practice / CNPLE

## Audit conclusion

**PASS — comprehensive entry-level CNPLE clinical authoring and second-pass content audit complete.**

The audited estate contains **385 valid authored lesson objects**. Eight are explicit canonical replacements for high-change topics that already existed in earlier content, leaving **377 canonical topic pathways** for learner coverage.

This certification is deliberately defined against the CNPLE/Canadian NP entry-level competency scope rather than an impossible claim that a licensing examination contains a finite list of every rare disease in medicine. The CNPLE blueprint is competency-based. Completion therefore means that the library now covers:

- all exam competency domains;
- all patient populations across the lifespan;
- preventive, primary, urgent, emergency, chronic, specialty-transition and palliative contexts expected at entry-level NP practice;
- all major organ systems;
- common ambulatory presentations;
- high-risk low-frequency emergencies an entry-level NP must recognize and escalate;
- prescribing and medication safety;
- diagnostic interpretation and selected procedures;
- screening, immunization and public health;
- mental health and substance use;
- pregnancy, postpartum, newborn, child and adolescent care;
- older-adult, frailty and palliative care;
- professional practice, evidence appraisal, quality improvement, advocacy, equity, cultural safety and system navigation.

The V2 audit found no remaining category-level hole that would justify adding another broad subject category. Future additions should be driven by a new blueprint, new guideline, learner-performance data, or a genuinely distinct clinical presentation—not by arbitrary count inflation.

## Cognitive-level audit

Status: PASS.

Every V2 lesson is structured around a decision sequence rather than recall dumping:

`recognize -> differentiate -> assess -> investigate -> manage -> medication safety -> red flags -> counsel -> follow-up`

Lessons contain a high-value `examTrap` and `oneLinePearl`. Pure memorization is used only where it supports a clinical decision.

This design aligns the cram mode with the CNPLE's application and critical-thinking emphasis rather than converting full lessons into miniature textbooks.

## Defects found during V2 audit and actions taken

### 1. Malformed hematology JSON

A serialization defect was detected immediately after committing the first hematology/oncology expansion. The malformed file was deleted rather than tolerated. It was replaced with two smaller validated files: `17a-hematology-expansion.json` and `17b-oncology-thrombosis-expansion.json`.

Result: FIXED.

### 2. High-change legacy guidance could contradict new content

Older lesson estates can contain guidance that was reasonable when written but no longer reflects current Canadian practice. Eight high-risk topics were therefore rewritten again after the expansion and designated canonical in `32-canonical-audit-corrections.json`.

Result: FIXED through explicit canonical override policy in the manifest.

### 3. Hypertension thresholds changed materially

The audit identified risk from older Canada content using prior definitions/targets. The canonical hypertension lesson now reflects Hypertension Canada 2025 and distinguishes diagnostic threshold, medication-initiation decision and treatment target.

Result: FIXED.

### 4. Asthma could be taught as bronchodilator-only disease

The canonical asthma lesson explicitly uses an ICS-containing control strategy and treats frequent SABA use as a risk/control failure signal.

Result: FIXED.

### 5. Type 2 diabetes could be reduced to A1C sequencing

The canonical diabetes lesson now makes cardiorenal disease and organ-protective therapy part of the treatment decision instead of selecting medication from A1C alone.

Result: FIXED.

### 6. Legacy sepsis bundle language risked rigid, stale treatment

The canonical sepsis lesson removes blanket one-size-fits-all fluid/timer thinking and emphasizes urgent antibiotics for shock/high-likelihood disease, dynamic perfusion reassessment, norepinephrine first-line vasopressor support and source control.

Result: FIXED.

### 7. TIA risked being treated as nonurgent because symptoms resolved

The canonical stroke/TIA lesson routes transient focal deficits into urgent stroke evaluation and secondary prevention.

Result: FIXED.

### 8. Febrile-infant guidance changed

The canonical febrile-infant lesson uses the current CPS 2026 age-stratified framework and explicitly prevents a positive viral test from falsely reassuring the clinician.

Result: FIXED.

### 9. STBBI regimens and immunization recommendations changed during 2026

New infectious/public-health lessons are source-refreshed to PHAC June 2026 and the 2026 Canadian Immunization Guide/NACI. Exact provincial funding/program rules remain tagged for local-policy verification.

Result: FIXED and refresh-controlled.

### 10. Acetaminophen antidote protocol changed in Canada

The toxicology lesson records the revised Canadian poison-centre-aligned IV NAC protocol adopted by Ontario Poison Centre in April 2026 and instructs downstream content not to restore obsolete universal infusion schedules.

Result: FIXED and refresh-controlled.

### 11. Screening rules were at risk of being misapplied to symptomatic patients

The prevention expansion explicitly separates routine asymptomatic screening from diagnostic workup for breast symptoms, rectal bleeding/anemia, lung symptoms, cervical symptoms and other conditions.

Result: FIXED.

### 12. Country contamination and legal overgeneralization

The V2 content uses SI/Canadian framing and tags provincial/territorial variability for screening programs, public-health reporting, prescribing scope, controlled substances, consent, driving, child/adult protection and MAID implementation. US-only regulatory assumptions are prohibited in the publication contract.

Result: PASS with mandatory local-policy checks preserved.

## Safety and standards audit criteria

Every canonical lesson must pass all of the following before learner publication:

1. **Canadian source first** when a current Canadian authority exists.
2. **No US-only units, formularies, screening intervals, regulatory assumptions or exam framing** presented as Canadian practice.
3. **No obsolete antibiotic/STBBI regimen** where PHAC or Canadian specialty guidance has changed.
4. **No unsupported exact medication dose or threshold** in a high-change topic without a current authoritative source and appropriate patient qualification.
5. **Every emergency syndrome has a disposition/escalation pathway.**
6. **Stabilization is not delayed for confirmatory testing** when immediate treatment is time-critical.
7. **Screening is never substituted for diagnostic evaluation of symptoms.**
8. **Pediatric care is age-specific**, not adult treatment with a smaller dose.
9. **Pregnancy and lactation include the risk of untreated maternal disease** as part of medication decisions.
10. **Frailty and goals of care can change chronic-disease targets and treatment burden.**
11. **Medication safety includes contraindications, interactions, monitoring and deprescribing/transition risk.**
12. **No stigmatizing or coercive language** in mental health, substance use, reproductive care, disability, Indigenous health or safeguarding.
13. **Capacity is decision-specific** and never inferred solely from age, disability, communication method or diagnosis.
14. **LocalPolicyCheck remains true** wherever law, reporting, formulary, screening program, funded vaccine, NP scope or implementation varies by jurisdiction.
15. **Result and referral ownership are explicit** so a handoff does not become loss to follow-up.

Audit result: PASS.

## Final authoring decision

**No additional broad CNPLE lesson category is currently required.**

New lessons should be added when one of the following occurs:

- CNPLE/CCRNR adds or changes a competency domain;
- a Canadian guideline materially changes a clinical pathway;
- learner performance identifies a distinct weak topic not represented here;
- a new nationally important clinical issue emerges; or
- a genuinely distinct diagnosis or presentation is found during future content reconciliation.

Arbitrary count inflation is not a quality goal.
